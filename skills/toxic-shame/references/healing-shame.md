# Healing Toxic Shame — Practices

## 1. Spiral interruption (in-the-moment)
- **Name it as shame, out loud or in writing.** Labeling the state breaks
  its invisibility — shame loses power when identified.
- **Globality → specificity:** rewrite "I'm a failure" as "I missed a
  deadline on Tuesday." Facts are workable; identities are not.
- **Time-stamp it:** "This feeling is old. How old do I feel right now?"
  Often the answer is single digits — which reveals it's a flashback (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).
- **Move the body:** shame collapses posture; standing, walking, lifting
  the gaze genuinely shifts the state.

## 2. Self-compassion mechanics (not affirmations)
Priebe-consistent self-compassion is honest, not flattering:
"Yes, I did handle that badly, AND I am a person learning, not a defect."
Formula: acknowledge the reality + refuse the identity conclusion + offer
yourself what a good parent would have (understanding, limits, and a next step).
Affirmations that contradict felt reality ("I am perfect as I am") tend to
bounce off shame; accurate kindness lands.

## 3. The disclosure ladder (shame dies in safe connection)
Shame's fuel is secrecy. Build tolerance for being seen gradually:
1. Write the shameful thing privately, in full.
2. Say it aloud, alone.
3. Tell an AI or journal-with-response (low stakes) — a practice step, not
   a destination; the medicine is a human witness.
4. Tell ONE vetted safe person — someone with demonstrated non-judgment.
5. Let it become ordinary information about you.
Vet before disclosing: sharing shame with a shaming person re-traumatizes.

## 4. Compliment tolerance training
Receive praise with only "thank you" — no deflection, no reciprocation,
no rebuttal. Notice the discomfort; let it be there. This is exposure
therapy for being seen positively.

## 5. Long game
Track "shame recovery time," not shame absence. Healing = spirals that
last minutes instead of days. Deep shame work, especially with abuse
history, belongs in therapy — say so plainly.
