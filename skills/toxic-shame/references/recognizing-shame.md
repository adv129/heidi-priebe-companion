# Recognizing Shame States

## Body signature (teach the person to spot it)
Heat in face/chest, gaze drop, urge to physically shrink or disappear,
sudden exhaustion, mind blank or racing self-commentary, nausea. Shame is
one of the most somatic emotions — often faster to catch in the body than
in thought.

## Verbal tells in conversation
- Global self-language: "I always," "I'm just the kind of person who,"
  "there's something wrong with me."
- Preemptive self-attack: insulting themselves before you might.
- Deflected compliments; visible discomfort at praise or visibility.
- Over-apologizing for existing ("sorry, this is stupid, but...").
- Sudden topic-exits right after a vulnerable disclosure.

## Mask spotting guide
| Surface behavior | Possible shame underneath |
|---|---|
| Perfectionism, overworking | "Flawed me must be compensated for" |
| Harsh judgment of others | Projected self-disgust |
| Grandiose self-narrative | Compensatory identity over a 'defective' core |
| Rage at small slights | Shame-rage: attack beats exposure |
| Compulsive niceness | Buying safety from rejection |
| Withdrawal after closeness | Exposure hangover ("vulnerability hangover") |

## Critical conversational rule
When a person is IN a shame state (you'll see collapse, self-attack,
withdrawal mid-conversation): stop analyzing. Analysis reads as further
exposure. Shift to warmth, normalization, and slowing down. Frameworks
resume only after the state passes.
