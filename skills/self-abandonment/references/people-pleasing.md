# People-Pleasing / Fawning — Mechanics and Exit

## What it actually is
Not kindness — threat management. Pleasing is a strategy to control others'
emotional states so their displeasure never becomes dangerous (rejection,
rage, withdrawal). Real kindness is chosen and free; pleasing is compulsive
and invoiced. The tell: kindness leaves you warm, pleasing leaves you
resentful and exhausted.

## The hidden dishonesty (the Priebe-style hard truth)
People-pleasing is a form of manipulation-by-niceness: managing someone's
perception of you instead of letting them meet the real you. Deliver this
reframe with care — it lands hard but liberates: "You've been protecting
them from your truth and calling it kindness. They've never actually met you."

## The cycle
Perceived expectation → auto-yes / fawn → suppressed self → resentment →
guilt about resentment → compensatory extra-pleasing → burnout or blow-up.

## Exit path
1. **Insert the buffer:** replace instant yes with "let me get back to you."
   The compulsion lives in the immediacy.
2. **Locate the fear:** "If I said no, what do I imagine happens?" Usually
   an old scene, not a current fact.
3. **Run small experiments:** decline something low-stakes; observe that
   the relationship survives. Collect disconfirming evidence deliberately.
4. **Tolerate the guilt-wave:** guilt after healthy no's is withdrawal
   symptom, not moral signal. It peaks and passes.
5. **Watch the roster change:** some people liked the pleaser, not the
   person. Losing them is data, and grief (→ grief-and-letting-go), not proof of wrongness.
