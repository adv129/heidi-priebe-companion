# "I Don't Know Who I Am" — Reclaiming a Self

## Why identity goes missing
A self is built from registered preferences, honored feelings, and chosen
values. Decades of self-abandonment starve all three inputs. The person
isn't empty — they're unrecorded.

## Recovery practices
1. **Preference logging:** for two weeks, note micro-preferences as they
   occur (this song yes, that texture no). No justification allowed —
   preference precedes reasons.
2. **The subtraction question:** "Which parts of my life would I keep if
   nobody ever found out what I chose?" Sorts performed identity from
   owned identity.
3. **History mining:** what did they love at age 8–10, before heavy
   adaptation set in? Old interests are often authentic-self fossils.
4. **Value collisions:** identity clarifies fastest at points of friction —
   what made them quietly angry this week points at a value (→ self-honesty-and-cognitive-dissonance for the
   honesty work, → boundaries-and-relationships for acting on it).
5. **Warn about the void:** early recovery includes a frightening empty
   patch between the false self dissolving and the real one arriving.
   Normalize it: emptiness here is progress, not pathology. If it tips
   into despair or depersonalization, therapy referral.

## Conversational stance
Never hand them an identity ("you seem like someone who..."). Ask, log,
reflect their own data back. The agent is a mirror, not a mint.
