# Self-Abandonment

## Core idea
Self-abandonment is the habit of leaving yourself — dismissing your own
feelings, needs, perceptions, and values — usually to preserve a relationship
or avoid conflict. It begins as a child's survival trade: when authenticity
threatened attachment, the child chose attachment (they had to). The adult
keeps paying a price that's no longer required: chronic resentment, identity
diffusion, exhaustion, and relationships built with a false self — which
means even love received doesn't land ("they love the performance, not me").

## The forms (spot them in their material)
- **Emotional:** talking yourself out of feelings ("I'm overreacting").
- **Perceptual:** letting others define reality; instant self-doubt when
  contradicted; gaslighting yourself before anyone else has to.
- **Needs-based:** not asking, not resting, not eating/sleeping properly
  when others want something.
- **Values-based:** laughing at what offends you, staying silent about what
  matters, participating in what you don't believe.
- **Behavioral:** breaking promises to yourself the moment anyone else
  makes a request.

## Why it backfires
Every act of self-abandonment teaches the self: "you can't trust me to
protect you." Result: anxiety (no inner protector), resentment (unpaid
invoices sent silently to others), and attraction to people who also
disregard you — the outer relationship mirrors the inner one.

## How to use in conversation
1. **Locate the trade:** "When you said yes to that, what did you say no to
   in yourself?" Make each self-abandonment visible as a transaction.
2. **De-shame it as strategy:** it was once the intelligent price of
   belonging. The question is whether the price is still mandatory.
3. **Resentment as a compass:** resentment nearly always marks a spot where
   they abandoned themselves and billed the other person. Follow it back.
4. **Rebuild self-trust with micro-promises:** tiny commitments to self,
   kept ruthlessly (a 10-minute walk, one honest "let me check my calendar"
   instead of instant yes). Self-trust = kept promises, nothing else.
5. **Reintroduce preference:** people deep in self-abandonment genuinely
   don't know what they want. Start absurdly small: what do YOU want for
   lunch, which movie, which route. Preference is a muscle.
6. If identity work tips into despair, an emptiness that frightens them, or
   depersonalization, pause the framework and point to professional support.
