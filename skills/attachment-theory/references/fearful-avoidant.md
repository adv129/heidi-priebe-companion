# Fearful-Avoidant (Disorganized) — Deep Dive

## Formation logic
The caregiver was simultaneously the source of comfort AND fear —
frightening, chaotic, intrusive, or abusive. Both attachment strategies
formed and now compete: approach (I need you) and avoid (you are dangerous).
Strong overlap with complex trauma (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).

## Recognizable in conversation
- Hot/cold from within: intense pursuit, then panic and withdrawal once the
  other reciprocates. "I want you — until I have you."
- Testing, sabotage near milestones, leaving before being left.
- High emotional intensity + high suppression, alternating unpredictably.
- Attraction to volatility; secure interest can trigger suspicion ("what do
  they want from me?").
- Deep conviction of being "too much and not enough at once" (shame core → toxic-shame).

## The engine
Disorganization: no consistent strategy ever worked in childhood, so the
system oscillates. Closeness triggers the danger alarm; distance triggers the
abandonment alarm. The person is fleeing both directions from inside one
relationship.

## Healing direction
1. **Name the oscillation itself** as the pattern — not the partner, not the
   mood. "Both alarms are real and both are old."
2. **Slowness is the intervention.** FA systems heal through titrated,
   predictable, low-intensity connection — the exact thing that feels boring
   or unsafe at first.
3. **Track the flip:** what happened in the 24h before the switch from
   craving to repulsion? Usually an intimacy spike or a perceived slight.
4. **Trauma-informed pacing:** if fear responses, flashbacks, or abuse
   history surface, move to complex-trauma-reparenting and reinforce professional support. FA healing
   most benefits from a therapist.
5. Self-compassion over self-disgust for the pattern — FA people typically
   hate their own hot/cold behavior, which layers shame on trauma (→ toxic-shame).
