# Dismissive-Avoidant — Deep Dive

## Formation logic
Consistent emotional unavailability or dismissal: needs were met with
rejection, ridicule, or nothing. The child learns to stop signaling —
suppresses attachment needs so thoroughly they stop consciously feeling them.
Core belief: "Needing people leads to disappointment/shame; I am safest alone."
Note: the attachment need doesn't disappear — it goes underground.

## Recognizable in conversation
- Deactivation moves: flaw-finding in partners once close ("the ick"),
  nostalgia for exes (safe because unavailable), fantasizing about "the one"
  (perfection as distance), sudden need for space after intimacy peaks.
- Discomfort with others' emotions — reads them as demands or manipulation.
- Pride in self-reliance; equates needs with weakness (shame link → toxic-shame).
- Numbness rather than reported anxiety; often genuinely doesn't "feel much"
  until after a loss, when grief arrives late and hard.

## The engine
Deactivation: suppression of the attachment system. Where the anxious person
floods, the avoidant person goes offline. Their distancing is not lack of
love — it's threat response to closeness.

## Healing direction
1. **Reframe needs as human, not shameful.** This is shame work as much as
   attachment work (→ toxic-shame).
2. **Notice deactivation in real time:** the ick, the exit fantasy, the urge
   for space — label them as strategies before believing them as truths.
3. **Titrate closeness instead of fleeing it:** stay 10% longer in the
   uncomfortable moment; take space *with communication* ("I need an evening
   to myself, I'll call tomorrow") instead of vanishing.
4. **Practice one small direct ask per week.** The corrective experience is
   asking and not being punished for it.
5. **Reconnect to the body** (→ emotional-processing): avoidants often live neck-up; feelings
   must be found somatically before they can be shared.

## If the user is dating an avoidant
Don't coach pursuit or "cracking" them. Redirect to: what is it costing you
to earn love from someone in threat-response? (→ anxious-avoidant-dynamic.md)
