# The Anxious-Avoidant Dynamic

## The trap as a system
Anxious pursuit triggers avoidant withdrawal; withdrawal triggers harder
pursuit. Each partner's strategy confirms the other's core fear. The
relationship stabilizes at a painful equilibrium: intermittent reinforcement,
which bonds the anxious partner harder (the slot-machine effect).

## Why it feels like passion
Activation is mistaken for chemistry. The anxious partner's nervous system
reads uncertainty as aliveness; the avoidant partner feels safest wanting
someone at a distance. Mutual secure interest, by contrast, feels "flat" to
both — at first.

## In conversation
- Map the loop with THEIR events: "You texted more → he went quieter → you
  escalated → he asked for space. See the circuit?"
- Remove the villain framing: two threat responses colliding, not a good
  person and a bad one.
- Key question for the anxious partner: "If nothing about them ever changes,
  is this the relationship you're choosing?"
- Key question for the avoidant partner: "What would you have to feel if you
  stayed in the room?"

## Exit vs. repair
Repair requires BOTH people naming the loop and working their own side —
one person cannot do couple-level regulation alone. If only one is working:
the honest options are acceptance of the pattern as-is, or grieving and
leaving (→ grief-and-letting-go). Do not prescribe which; illuminate the trade-offs.
