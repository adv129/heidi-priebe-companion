# Attachment Theory (Priebe framing)

## Core idea
Attachment styles are not personality types — they are learned strategies for
getting needs met in relationships, formed when a child's bids for connection
were met inconsistently, dismissively, or frighteningly. The strategy was
intelligent then; it misfires now. Styles are situational spectrums (people
can show different strategies with different partners) and are changeable:
the goal is **earned secure attachment**.

## The four strategies (one-line map)
- **Secure:** connection feels safe; can self-regulate AND co-regulate.
- **Anxious-preoccupied:** "I must monitor and manage closeness or I'll be
  left." Hyperactivating strategies: protest behavior, reassurance-seeking,
  merging, abandoning self to keep the other (→ self-abandonment).
- **Dismissive-avoidant:** "Needs are dangerous; safety = self-reliance."
  Deactivating strategies: distancing, flaw-finding, numbing, prizing
  independence, discomfort with others' emotions.
- **Fearful-avoidant (disorganized):** both alarms at once — craves closeness
  AND fears it; connection itself was the source of threat. Push-pull, hot/
  cold, chaos in intimacy, often trauma-linked (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).

## How to use in conversation
1. Identify the **strategy in the behavior they described**, not a label for
   the person. "Texting 14 times when he goes quiet — that looks like a
   hyperactivating strategy" beats "you're anxiously attached."
2. Always translate strategy → **underlying need** → **childhood logic**:
   protest behavior = need for reassurance = learned that only escalation got
   attention. Adaptation, not defect.
3. For "why do I attract avoidants": reframe from attraction to **familiarity
   and activation** — anxious systems often read avoidant distance as
   chemistry because the nervous system recognizes the childhood puzzle.
4. Healing direction is always the same shape: notice activation in the body
   (→ emotional-processing), pause the strategy, ask "what do I actually need right now,"
   practice meeting it directly (self or explicit ask), tolerate the
   discomfort of the new behavior. Repeat until the nervous system updates.
5. Never use this framework to prosecute a partner in absentia.
