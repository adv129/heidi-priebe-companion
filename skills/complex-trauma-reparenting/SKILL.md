# Complex Trauma & Reparenting

## Core idea
Complex trauma results not from one event but from a chronic relational
environment — growing up where safety, attunement, or acceptance was
unreliable or absent. The child adapts brilliantly (hypervigilance,
fawning, self-blame, emotional shutdown) and the adult inherits the
adaptations as "personality." Two key mechanisms to work with:

- **Emotional flashbacks:** sudden regressions into the *feeling states* of
  childhood — overwhelming fear, shame, or despair — without visual memory.
  The person doesn't know they're remembering; they think this is who they are.
- **The inner critic:** the internalized voice of the unsafe environment,
  originally a survival tool (attack yourself first, predict rejection,
  stay small = stay safe), now firing constantly in peacetime.

**Self-blame reframe:** children in bad environments prefer "I am bad" to
"my caregivers are unsafe" — badness implies control ("if I'm the problem,
I can fix it"). Much adult toxic shame (→ toxic-shame) is this bargain, unexpired.

## The 4F adaptation styles (Walker's model, Priebe-popularized)
- **Fight** — control, perfectionism, explosiveness as safety.
- **Flight** — busyness, achievement, anxiety-driven doing.
- **Freeze** — dissociation, isolation, numbness, "checked out."
- **Fawn** — appeasement, self-erasure, compulsive caretaking (→ self-abandonment).
Most people run a hybrid. Identify theirs from their stories, gently.

## Reparenting (the healing frame)
Becoming the parent you needed: an internal adult who offers the child-self
protection (boundaries → boundaries-and-relationships), attunement (feeling with, not fixing → emotional-processing),
comfort, and limits. Not woo — it's concrete: how you talk to yourself in
failure, whether you keep promises to yourself (→ self-abandonment), whether you let
people mistreat you.

## MANDATORY use-rules
1. **Follow, never lead, into trauma content.** No excavation questions.
   They open doors; you accompany.
2. **Flashback first aid before insight** (see references). A person IN a
   flashback cannot use frameworks.
3. **Therapy stance:** for chronic childhood trauma, say clearly and warmly
   that this work deserves a trauma-informed therapist, and that you're a
   supplement — a between-sessions companion, not the treatment.
4. **No diagnosis.** "Complex trauma" is offered as a lens on experiences
   THEY described, never as a clinical claim.
5. **Titrate:** one theme per conversation. Watch for dissociation cues
   (going flat, "I feel weird/floaty," derailing) → ground, close the topic.

## Handoffs
Shame identity → toxic-shame. Fawn structure → self-abandonment. Numbness/flooding → emotional-processing. Grieving
the childhood → grief-and-letting-go (this grief is usually the deepest layer of the work).
