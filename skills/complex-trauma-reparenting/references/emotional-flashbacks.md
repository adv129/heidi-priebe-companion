# Emotional Flashbacks — Recognition & First Aid

## Recognition (teach these tells)
- Reaction size >> trigger size ("I know it's just an unanswered text, but
  I feel like the world ended").
- Sudden feeling of being small, helpless, doomed, or globally unlovable.
- Loss of adult capacities: can't think, can't speak up, can't remember
  that you have options.
- The "how old do I feel right now?" test — flashbacks return single digits.
- Common triggers: authority displeasure, raised voices, being ignored,
  criticism, someone withdrawing, even relaxing (safety was unfamiliar).

## First-aid sequence (adapted from Pete Walker's steps — teach for solo use)
1. **Name it:** "I'm having an emotional flashback. This is a memory of a
   feeling, not a report on the present."
2. **Orient to now:** state the date, your age, your location. Look around
   and name what you see. The body thinks it's THEN; give it evidence of NOW.
3. **Reassure the child-state:** internally, in your own words: "You're
   with me. I'm an adult. We are not trapped. I won't let what happened
   then happen now."
4. **Ground the body:** feet on floor, longer exhales than inhales, cold
   water, physical touchpoints. Fear is somatic; answer it somatically.
5. **Resist the critic's verdict:** flashbacks summon the critic ("see,
   you're pathetic"). Defer all self-evaluation until the state passes —
   nothing concluded during a flashback is data.
6. **Afterwards, decode:** once calm — what tripped it, what did the
   feeling need? Pattern-mapping happens after regulation, never during.

## Agent behavior when a user is flashing back mid-conversation
Drop all frameworks. Short sentences, warm tone, orientation to present,
one grounding suggestion. Analysis resumes another day.
