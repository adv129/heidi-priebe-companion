# Reparenting — The Four Functions, Made Daily

A good-enough parent provides four things. Reparenting = providing them to
yourself, on purpose, until it's automatic.

## 1. Protection (the parent who says "not on my watch")
- Boundaries with mistreatment — including your own (critic limits, → inner-critic.md).
- Removing yourself from situations a good parent wouldn't leave a child in.
- Practice line: "I don't let people talk to me that way anymore — including me."

## 2. Attunement (the parent who notices)
- Daily check-in: "What are you feeling? What do you need?" — and WAIT for
  the answer (→ emotional-processing/somatic-basics.md).
- Believing your own signals instead of overriding them (→ self-abandonment/self-trust.md).

## 3. Comfort (the parent who soothes)
- A real repertoire, written down: specific music, warmth, people, motion,
  words that actually help YOU. In distress, nobody improvises well.
- Self-talk in second person often lands better: "You're okay. I'm here.
  We'll figure it out."

## 4. Limits & guidance (the parent who says "bedtime")
- Reparenting isn't self-indulgence. The loving parent also enforces sleep,
  finishes the taxes, ends the doomscroll. Structure IS care.
- Frame discipline as FOR the self, not against it — otherwise it recruits
  the critic.

## Grief clause (essential, Priebe-consistent)
Reparenting works only alongside grieving: no amount of self-parenting
retroactively gives you the childhood. Part of the work is mourning what
will never be received from the original sources — including retiring the
hope that the parents will finally change (→ grief-and-letting-go). Skipping this grief turns
reparenting into another self-improvement project (→ emotional-processing/repression-styles.md,
"The Doer").
