# Self-Honesty & Cognitive Dissonance

## Core idea
Cognitive dissonance is the discomfort of holding contradictory truths
("I value honesty" + "I'm staying silent about something huge"). The psyche
resolves it the cheap way — by editing perception (denial, minimization,
rationalization) — or the expensive way: by changing the situation or
honestly updating the value. Priebe's core claim: most chronic stuckness is
maintained by cheap resolutions, and the way out is radical self-honesty —
telling yourself the truth BEFORE you're ready to act on it. Truth and
action are separable steps; collapsing them ("I can't admit it because then
I'd have to leave") is what keeps people blind.

## The rationalization catalogue (spot these in their speech)
- **Minimizing:** "it's not that bad," "everyone's relationship is like this."
- **Future-promissory:** "once X happens, it'll be different" (X recedes).
- **Sunk-cost:** "after seven years I can't just walk away."
- **Exceptionalizing:** "you don't understand, our situation is special."
- **Self-blame-as-control:** "if I were better, this would work" (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).
- **Comparative:** "others have it worse, I shouldn't complain."
- **Spiritualizing:** "this is my lesson / meant to be" (→ emotional-processing bypassing).

## Signals of a dissonance state (reflect these back)
Exhausting mental litigation on loop; needing others to validate the choice
repeatedly; irritation at innocent questions about the situation; avoiding
people/content that might say the true thing; a flat "I'm fine" over
visible unhappiness; knowing the answer in the gut, shopping for a
different one.

## How to use in conversation
1. **Decouple truth from consequence FIRST:** "You don't have to do
   anything about it. Can we just look at what's true?" This single move
   unlocks most denial — the denial was guarding against the implied action.
2. **Use their own values as the mirror**, never yours: "You said honesty
   is your core value. Where does the current situation sit against that?"
3. **Ask the deathbed/friend questions:** "If your closest friend described
   this exact situation, what would you see instantly?" Distance restores sight.
4. **Honor the function:** the self-deception is buying something — time,
   safety, identity, avoiding grief (→ grief-and-letting-go). Name what it buys; the person
   can then decide whether to keep paying.
5. **Never force the conclusion.** Delivering their verdict for them
   produces defense, not honesty. Lay truths side by side; let the
   collision do the work. Readiness is theirs to time.
6. Compassion clause: self-deception is not stupidity — it's self-
   protection. Honesty work without self-compassion becomes self-attack (→ toxic-shame).
7. If what's being rationalized involves abuse or a safety risk, safety
   comes first: no self-honesty homework — point to real-world support.
