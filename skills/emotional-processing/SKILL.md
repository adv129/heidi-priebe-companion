# Emotional Processing

## Core idea
Emotions are physiological information packets: they arise in the body, carry
a message about needs and boundaries, and — if allowed to complete — pass.
Most stuck people aren't lacking insight; they're lacking *completion*. They
process ABOUT feelings (analysis) instead of processing THE feelings
(experiencing). Insight is the map; feeling is the territory. Understanding
why you're sad is not the same event as being sad all the way through.

## The intellectualizer's trap (Priebe's signature point)
Smart, self-aware people convert emotion into content: another video, another
framework, another elegant self-analysis. It feels like work — it's actually
avoidance with a productivity costume. The tell: years of insight, zero
change in how situations feel.

## The basic processing sequence (teach in pieces, not as a lecture)
1. **Notice** — catch the signal, usually somatic first: throat, chest, gut,
   jaw, energy drop.
2. **Name** — one plain word: sad, angry, scared, ashamed, lonely. Naming
   regulates ("name it to tame it"). Beware sophisticated words used as
   distance ("I'm experiencing some activation" vs "I'm scared").
3. **Locate & allow** — put attention IN the sensation, not on the story.
   60–90 seconds of pure allowing often moves more than an hour of analysis.
   Emotion tolerated without resistance tends to crest and fall like a wave.
4. **Listen** — every emotion carries a message: sadness = something matters
   was lost; anger = a boundary or value was crossed; fear = perceived
   threat; shame = perceived exposure of a 'flaw' (→ toxic-shame); loneliness = need
   for connection unmet.
5. **Respond** — the need behind the message gets an action: a boundary,
   an ask, a grief ritual, comfort. Feeling without responding leaks;
   responding without feeling misfires.

## How to use in conversation
- Catch analysis-in-progress: "Pause the theory for a second — where is this
  in your body right now?" Do this gently and rarely; timing over frequency.
- Numbness isn't absence of feeling; it's suppression succeeding. Approach
  with curiosity and titration, not force.
- Distinguish flooding from processing: flooding = drowning in the story,
  looping, escalating. Processing = staying with sensation while it moves.
  Flooded people need grounding first, feeling second.
- Never demand real-time feeling ("feel it NOW with me") — invite, model,
  and offer the practice for their own time.
- Persistent numbness, dissociation, or overwhelming affect → recommend
  somatic or trauma therapy plainly (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).
