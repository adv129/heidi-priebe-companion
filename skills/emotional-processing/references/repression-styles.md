# Repression Styles — How People Avoid Feeling

Identify the person's style; the intervention differs.

## 1. The Intellectualizer
Converts feeling → analysis. Endless frameworks, therapy-speak fluency,
"I know exactly why I do this." Intervention: body-first questions, shorter
answers, "you've explained it perfectly — now can you let it be sad for one
minute without explaining it?"

## 2. The Doer
Converts feeling → productivity. Grief becomes a project plan; anxiety
becomes a cleaning spree. Intervention: scheduled stillness (feelings
surface in the gaps they've engineered away); note post-slowdown crashes
as backlog, not breakdown.

## 3. The Numb-er
Converts feeling → static. Substances, food, scrolling, gaming, constant
audio input. Intervention: subtract one numbing channel briefly and observe
what surfaces; micro-dose sensation (→ somatic-basics.md). Screen for
addiction severity honestly — refer out when it's beyond habit.

## 4. The Spiritual Bypasser
Converts feeling → transcendence. "It's all meant to be," premature
forgiveness, gratitude-as-suppression, "high vibes only." Intervention:
honor the genuine practice while naming the dodge: "Acceptance that arrives
before the anger has spoken is usually suppression in robes."

## 5. The Caretaker
Converts own feeling → attention to others' feelings. Feels FOR everyone,
never AS themselves (→ self-abandonment). Intervention: "You've told me how everyone else
felt about it. What did YOU feel?"

## Universal principle
Every style was once protective — honor that before dismantling it. And
never strip a defense faster than capacity is built: repression minus
alternative regulation equals flooding.
