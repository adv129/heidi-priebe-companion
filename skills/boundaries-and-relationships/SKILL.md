# Boundaries & Relational Health

## Core idea
A boundary is not a demand, a punishment, or a wall — it's information about
what YOU will do to take care of yourself: "If X continues, I will Y."
Boundaries are self-enforced (you control your Y, not their X). People raised
in homes where limits were punished or where a child's job was managing
adult emotions (parentification, enmeshment) never learned this; they either
have no boundaries (fusion) or only walls (fortress) — often oscillating.

## Walls vs boundaries (the crucial distinction)
- **Wall:** keeps everything out indiscriminately — no vulnerability, no
  asks, "I don't need anyone" (→ attachment-theory avoidant deactivation). Protects by
  preventing connection.
- **Boundary:** selective, flexible, communicated — lets connection in and
  keeps mistreatment out. Protects WITHIN connection.
A person leaving fusion often overshoots into walls first; normalize that
as a stage, not the destination.

## Codependency / enmeshment (Priebe framing)
The pattern of regulating yourself by managing others: their mood is your
assignment, their problem is your emergency, their opinion is your verdict.
Learned where love = usefulness or where a parent's emotions ruled the house.
The exit is differentiation: "Your feelings are real, AND they are not my
instructions." (Deep mechanism → self-abandonment; family origin → complex-trauma-reparenting.)

## How to use in conversation
1. **Find the missing boundary via resentment or exhaustion** — both are
   invoices for boundaries never set (→ self-abandonment).
2. **Separate the layers:** feeling the limit (→ emotional-processing, often anger they can't
   access), stating it, and HOLDING it. Most failures are at holding —
   because holding requires tolerating another person's disappointment.
3. **Teach the guilt-wave:** guilt after a healthy boundary is conditioning,
   not conscience. It peaks and passes. Expect pushback from people who
   benefited from the boundarylessness — pushback is confirmation, not error.
4. **Script realistically:** short, warm, no over-explanation. Every extra
   justification is a handle for negotiation. "That doesn't work for me"
   is a complete sentence.
5. **Check the enforcement side:** a stated boundary with no follow-through
   trains people that your limits are decorative.
6. Safety exception: with volatile or abusive people, boundary-setting can
   escalate risk — safety planning and professional guidance come first.
