# Healthy Conflict & Repair

## Reframe
Conflict-free relationships aren't healthy ones — they're usually
self-abandoning ones (→ self-abandonment) or distant ones. Security is built not by
avoiding rupture but by surviving rupture-and-repair cycles. Repair, not
harmony, is the trust mechanism.

## Fighting clean (teachable basics)
- One issue at a time; no kitchen-sinking, no archive-diving.
- Complaints about behavior, not character: "when X happened, I felt Y"
  vs "you always / you're so."
- Time-outs WITH return times ("I need 30 minutes, then let's finish") —
  a pause with a comeback is regulation; a pause without one is stonewalling
  (→ attachment-theory deactivation).
- Flooded = offline: past a certain arousal level nobody processes; pursue
  resolution later, regulation now (→ emotional-processing).
- Curiosity clause: somewhere in every fight, ask one real question about
  their experience. Fights de-escalate when someone gets curious first.

## The repair sequence
1. Re-approach without relitigating ("I don't like where we ended up").
2. Own YOUR piece specifically — no "I'm sorry you felt," no asterisks.
3. Hear their impact without defense (defense converts repair into round two).
4. Name the next-time plan.
5. Reconnect (warmth, humor, touch where appropriate) — repair completes
   in the body, not just the transcript.

## Repair with self
After the person behaves badly in conflict: same sequence internally —
own it, understand the trigger (often a flashback → complex-trauma-reparenting), plan, and refuse
the shame spiral (→ toxic-shame). People who can't self-repair either over-apologize
(fawn) or can't apologize at all (shame armor); both trace to the same root.
