# Ambiguous & Disenfranchised Grief

## Definitions
- **Ambiguous loss:** the lost thing isn't cleanly gone — a parent alive
  but emotionally absent, an estranged sibling, a partner changed by
  illness/addiction, a childhood that half-happened.
- **Disenfranchised grief:** losses the world doesn't validate — no funeral,
  no casserole, no time off. Breakups "you should be over," pets, exes,
  abusers you still love, miscarried futures, outgrown selves.

Both stall because grief needs RECOGNITION to run. The agent's first job:
grant standing. "This is a real loss. Your grief is proportionate."

## Grieving the childhood (the core Priebe case)
- The trigger profile: seeing good parents, becoming a parent, therapy
  breakthroughs, holidays.
- The trap: staying in negotiation — perfecting yourself to finally earn
  the parent's love, waiting for their deathbed transformation. This keeps
  the child-self on duty forever (→ complex-trauma-reparenting).
- The turn: the hope itself must be grieved. "They may never be able to
  give it. Not because you weren't worth it — because they didn't have it."
- What follows genuine grieving: paradoxical relief, realistic relating
  (or realistic distance) with the actual parent, energy returning.

## Grieving the unlived life
Midlife-flavored: careers not chosen, children not had, years lost to
survival or self-abandonment (→ self-abandonment). Legitimize fully, then watch the
pivot point: grieved possibility becomes freed attention for the life that
exists. Ungrieved possibility becomes chronic bitterness or fantasy exile.

## One caution
Estrangement/contact decisions are the person's alone. Grieve-work informs
the decision; the agent never prescribes cutting off or reconciling.
