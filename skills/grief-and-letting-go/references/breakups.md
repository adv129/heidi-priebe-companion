# Breakups — Deep Dive

## Why it hurts this much (normalize with mechanism)
Attachment bonds are physiological; a breakup is withdrawal from a
regulating presence. The brain treats the ex like a missing limb and fires
search behavior: checking their socials, replaying, "accidental" contact.
This is biology, not weakness or a sign it was "meant to be."

## The double loss
Grieve BOTH: the actual relationship (with its real problems) and the
imagined future / idealized partner. Stuck people are usually mourning the
fantasy — which can't die because it never lived. Restore accuracy gently:
"Tell me about the parts that didn't work" belongs in the conversation.

## No-contact (frame as detox, not strategy)
Contact resets the withdrawal clock and feeds intermittent-reinforcement
bonding (→ attachment-theory, anxious-avoidant dynamic). No-contact is for nervous-system
recovery — NOT a maneuver to be missed. If they're doing it to provoke
pursuit, that's protest behavior; name it kindly.

## Rumination traps
- **The autopsy loop:** infinite "why." Offer: you can have understanding
  or you can have peace on a deadline — full closure from the other person
  is a myth; closure is something you build, not receive.
- **The chance-ghost:** "if I'd just said X." Counter: patterns, not
  moments, end relationships.
- **Checking behavior:** each check = a re-injury. Treat like any
  abstinence problem: blockers, substitution, urge-surfing (→ emotional-processing).

## Identity rebuild
Especially post-enmeshment (→ self-abandonment): the "we" hosted the self. Rebuild with
preference-logging, reclaimed friendships, and one old abandoned interest.
The acute phase typically eases in weeks-to-months with real grieving;
flag stalled cases (year+ with no movement, or functioning collapse) to
professional support.
