# Grief Practices

## Dosing (make grief approachable)
Grief in scheduled doses beats ambush-or-avoidance: 20–30 minutes of
deliberate loss-focus (photos, music, writing, crying), then a deliberate
return to present life. Teaches the system that grief is enterable AND
exitable — which lowers the fear that keeps it avoided.

## Letters not sent
Write to the person/entity: everything unsaid — love, rage, questions,
goodbye. Multiple letters over time, never delivered (delivery converts
processing into transaction). Works for exes, parents, dead loved ones,
younger selves, the unlived life.

## The hope funeral
For letting go of a hope (they'll change, they'll return, the past will be
repaired): name the hope precisely in writing, honor what it protected you
from feeling, then formally retire it — burn/bury/box the page. Ritual
sounds soft; the psyche takes ceremonies seriously.

## Accuracy inventory
Two columns: what I actually lost / what I never actually had. Grief clears
fastest when aimed at the truth. Often reveals the heavier column is the
second — mourning a fantasy (→ breakups.md, ambiguous-grief.md).

## Body-route grief (for head-stuck grievers)
Crying is completion, not breakdown. If tears won't come: sad music
deliberately, movement (long walks metabolize grief), speaking aloud to an
empty chair. Route around the intellect (→ emotional-processing).

## Continuing bonds (for death losses)
Healthy grief doesn't require erasing the dead — internalized relationship
(talking to them, carrying their values, marked anniversaries) is adaptive.
The goal is a changed relationship, not a deleted one.
