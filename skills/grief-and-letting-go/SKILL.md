# Grief & Letting Go

## Core idea
Grief is not an obstacle to healing — it IS the healing mechanism. It's how
the psyche updates its internal world to match a changed external reality.
Most "stuckness" (rumination, can't move on, bitterness, chronic hope for a
different past) is unfinished grief: the mind refusing to run the update
because the update hurts. You cannot skip grief; you can only defer it,
and deferred grief charges interest (numbness, cynicism, repetition of the
pattern with new people).

## The kinds people don't recognize as grief (Priebe's key contribution)
- **The childhood you didn't have** — mourning safety, attunement, delight
  that never came (→ complex-trauma-reparenting). Often triggered by seeing good parenting.
- **The parent who exists but can't** — grieving a living person's
  limitations; retiring the hope that they'll finally become who you needed.
- **The relationship you thought you were in** — post-breakup grief is often
  double: the person AND the imagined future/the person you thought they were.
- **The potential self** — years lost to survival mode, paths not taken,
  the person you'd have been with different luck.
- **Growth grief** — outgrowing friends, identities, coping mechanisms,
  even beliefs. Every level of healing has an exit toll paid in grief.

## Working truths
- Stages aren't linear; grief is oscillation (waves), and dosing between
  loss-focus and restoration-focus is healthy, not avoidance.
- **Rumination vs grieving:** rumination loops in the head (why, what-if,
  re-litigating); grieving moves through the body (crying, aching,
  releasing). Rumination is often grief-avoidance wearing grief's clothes —
  redirect from story to feeling (→ emotional-processing).
- **Idealization check:** you can't release what you won't see accurately.
  Gently restore the full picture of what was lost — including its problems.
- **Hope is the last thing to grieve.** "Letting go" usually means letting
  go of the hope (they'll come back / they'll change / the past can be fixed),
  and that's the cut that finally frees.

## How to use in conversation
1. Fresh loss (days/weeks): presence over frameworks. Witness, normalize
   waves, no silver linings, no timelines.
2. Stuck loss (months/years): look for the ungrieved element — usually a
   hope still on life support, or an ambiguous loss never named AS a loss.
   Naming it as grief is often the intervention: "That's not weakness.
   That's grief — and it makes sense."
3. Anger, relief, and love can coexist in one loss. Permission for the
   "wrong" feelings (relief that a hard parent died; missing an abuser)
   unlocks stalled grief.
4. Watch for prolonged, functioning-destroying grief or grief+hopelessness →
   professional support, said directly.
